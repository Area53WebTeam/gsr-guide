★ A Word From Your Chairperson ▼

Welcome to general service. As a GSR, you carry your group's voice into the Area — and that's no small thing. Our structure runs upside down from most organizations: the groups hold the final authority, and the Assembly exists to serve you, not the other way around (Tradition Two; Concept I).

This guide is meant to take the mystery out of Assembly business — how an idea gets onto the agenda, how a motion is made, and how we listen to one another, even in disagreement. None of it is complicated once you've seen it done a time or two. My hope is that reading through it once will make you comfortable enough to stand up at the microphone the next time something matters to you and your group.

In love and service,
Your Area 53 Chairperson

1 Why We Do Business This Way ▼

In A.A., a business meeting is still a spiritual gathering. Parliamentary procedure isn't a contest of rules — it's a trusted servant in its own right, there to keep things orderly, fair, and unhurried enough that everyone gets heard.

A few principles underneath all of it

Tradition Two: "For our group purpose there is but one ultimate authority — a loving God as He may express Himself in our group conscience. Our leaders are but trusted servants; they do not govern."
Concepts I & II: Final responsibility and the ultimate authority for A.A. world services belongs to the groups — and that authority flows through the Conference, right down to our Area Assembly.
Concept IV — Right of Participation: Every voting member, especially each GSR, has an equal right to speak, vote, and be heard.
Concept XII, Warranty Four: "That all important decisions be reached by discussion, vote, and, whenever possible, by substantial unanimity."
Concept XII, Warranty Five: "That no Conference [or Assembly] action ever be personally punitive or an incitement to public controversy."

Source: The A.A. Service Manual combined with Twelve Concepts for World Service.

2 Who Holds the Authority? ▼

A frequent point of confusion is who actually governs in Area 53. Simply put:

  • The Area Assembly holds ultimate authority. Made up of GSRs, DCMs, Officers, and Standing Committee Chairs, the Assembly is our final group conscience. It elects officers, approves guidelines, passes the budget, and decides broad policy.
  • The Area Committee serves the Assembly. Between Assemblies, the Committee (Officers, Standing Chairs, and DCMs) coordinates administration, hears progress reports, and helps the Area Chair prepare the Assembly agenda (Area 53 Guidelines, Article VII).
  • The Area Committee does not govern the Assembly. It cannot veto business, change guidelines, or substitute its judgment for the full Assembly's conscience. GSRs hold the majority of votes in the Assembly — you are the voice of A.A. in Central and Southeastern Ohio.
3 Getting Your Idea to the Floor ▼

The Area Assembly agenda is prepared by the Area Chairperson with the Area Committee and sent to all members at least one month before the Assembly meets (Area 53 Guidelines, Article VI §3).

The usual way: through your district

  1. Bring your idea or motion to your home group for a group conscience.
  2. Share it with your District Committee Member (DCM) at your monthly District meeting.
  3. Your DCM carries it to the Area Committee meeting for a spot on the upcoming Assembly agenda.

If your district has no active DCM

An unrepresented or inactive district doesn't mean your group goes unheard. You can:

  • Write to the Area Chairperson or Area Secretary directly, with your motion and some background, well before the Area Committee meets.
  • Reach out to the DCM Chairperson (DCMC), who represents districts on the Area Committee and helps unrepresented ones move business forward.
  • Connect with Group Services or a Standing Committee — if your item touches Treatment, Corrections, Accessibilities, PI/CPC, or Website/Tech, that chair can help introduce it during agenda planning.

From the floor, on Assembly day

Missed the one-month notice? You still have options under parliamentary procedure:

  • Amend the agenda: when the Chair presents the draft agenda for adoption, rise and move, "I move to amend the agenda to add [topic] under New Business." (Needs a second, is debatable, 2/3 majority.)
  • Make a motion under New Business: once the Assembly reaches New Business on the adopted agenda, any voting GSR may stand and offer an original main motion.
4 Plain-Language Motions ▼

Area 53 uses a simplified version of Robert's Rules, in the same spirit as the General Service Conference's own practice (Service Manual, Appendix W). Search or scan below for the most common situations:

Introduce a new action or policy
"I move that [exact action]..."
Second? Yes Debatable? Yes Vote needed: Two-thirds*
Change the wording of a motion on the floor
"I move to amend the motion by adding/striking..."
Second? Yes Debatable? Yes Vote needed: Two-thirds
Give GSRs time to check with their groups
"I move to table the motion until next Assembly"
Second? Yes Debatable? No Vote needed: Two-thirds
Send an idea to a committee for more work
"I move to refer to [committee]"
Second? Yes Debatable? Yes Vote needed: Majority
End debate and vote right away
"I call the question" (must be recognized first — you cannot speak, then call it)
Second? Yes Debatable? No Vote needed: Two-thirds
Ask a clarifying or procedural question
"Point of Order" or "Point of Information"
Second? No Debatable? No Vote needed: Chair decides

No matches — try a different word, or check with your Chair.

*Two-thirds majority is Area 53's everyday practice for ordinary business. A two-thirds vote is also required to amend the Area 53 Guidelines themselves (Article XIV) and for budget items over $300 that need full Assembly approval. If you're not sure which threshold applies to a particular motion, ask your Chair or the Guidelines Committee before the vote — don't guess at the mic.

5 Substantial Unanimity ▼

Unlike a political body where 51% carries the day, A.A. strives for substantial unanimity — a two-thirds vote, creating harmony and consensus, not a bare win. This is also where we make room to hear from members who voted "no," even after the gavel falls.

Hearing the minority

Chair
After a vote is tallied — whichever way it went — asks: "Does anyone who voted on the minority side wish to speak?"
↓
Minority side only
Members who voted with the minority may share their reasons.
↓
Whole assembly
No debate follows. The Assembly simply listens, in silence, to be sure no viewpoint got overlooked.

Motion to reconsider

Chair
After the minority is heard, asks: "Does anyone who voted on the prevailing side wish to move to reconsider?"
↓
Prevailing side only
Only someone persuaded by what the minority said may make this motion.
↓
If seconded
A simple majority reopens debate. If that passes, the floor is open again before a final vote.

This mirrors how the General Service Conference itself handles minority opinion (Service Manual, Appendix W, and Concept V). Area 53's own Guidelines call for a two-thirds vote specifically to amend the Guidelines (Article XIV); for everything else, check with your Chair on what threshold your Assembly has adopted.

✦ A Closing Word ▼

Never hesitate to step to the microphone. Whether you're a first-time GSR or a seasoned trusted servant, your voice carries the heartbeat of your group into Area 53. When we practice open discussion, patience, and love for the Fellowship, our better judgment — call it group conscience, call it something greater — has room to speak through us.

This guide is offered as a helpful reference from the Chairperson's desk, not as Area 53 policy. Where it touches procedure that isn't spelled out in the Area 53 Guidelines or the A.A. Service Manual, please check with the Chairperson, Delegate, or the relevant committee rather than relying on this alone — and any wording changes to this guide belong to the group conscience of those who use it.
